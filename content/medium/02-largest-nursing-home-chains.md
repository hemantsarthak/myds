# America's largest nursing-home chains, counted from CMS data

*Draft for Medium.*

---

Medicare records the chain behind every nursing home. Counting facilities by chain turns a fragmented industry into a surprisingly concentrated one: a handful of operators run thousands of buildings, and the ten largest chains account for a large share of all chain-affiliated facilities.

From the CMS Nursing Home Provider Information dataset: **10,184 chain-affiliated facilities across 611 chains**. The largest operators run hundreds of homes each.

## Why chain size matters for families

Chain affiliation is not a quality measure — CMS rates each building separately — but it is a practical thing to know:

- **Staffing policy is often set at chain level.** Nurse hours per resident and turnover tend to move together across a chain's buildings.
- **Problems repeat.** When a chain has systemic issues, inspection deficiencies show up across its portfolio.
- **CMS publishes chain-level averages** for exactly this reason: the chain average rating, health-inspection rating, staffing rating and quality-measure rating are all in the public data.

The full ranking — the 20 largest chains by facility count, with certified beds and average CMS star rating — is here: [largest nursing-home chains](https://tekify.xyz/insights/largest-nursing-home-chains/).

## What the star rating does and does not tell you

The overall rating combines health inspections, staffing and quality measures. For families, **staffing is usually the most informative of the three**: it covers nurse hours per resident per day and how often staff turn over. High turnover is a warning sign even when headline hours look adequate.

What the rating does not capture: food, activities, the atmosphere of the building, or whether the home accepts your family member's insurance. Those need a visit, a meal and questions.
