How to compare US colleges on net price, not sticker price

Sticker price tells you little. Average net price — tuition and living costs minus grant aid — is the number that matters, and the gap between colleges is large.

US colleges publish a sticker price that almost nobody pays. The number that matters is the average net price: tuition, fees, books and living costs minus grant and scholarship aid. It is what students actually pay, and it varies enormously between colleges with similar sticker prices.

What to compare

  • Net price by family income band. A college can be expensive on average and cheap for low-income students, or the reverse.
  • Graduation and retention rates. A cheap college that students do not finish is expensive in the end.
  • In-state vs out-of-state tuition at public universities — the difference can be tens of thousands a year.

Where to find it

The free US Colleges directory lists 6,056 institutions with acceptance rates, tuition, average net price by income band, graduation and retention rates, all from federal IPEDS data. Compare a shortlist side by side rather than relying on a single ranking.
